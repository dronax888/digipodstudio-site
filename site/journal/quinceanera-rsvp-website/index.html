How to collect quinceañera RSVPs online with a website

By Digipod Studio, November 9, 2026

Counting guests is one of the least glamorous parts of planning a quinceañera, and one of the most important. The caterer, the venue, the tables and the favors all depend on one number. Replies scattered across texts, WhatsApp and phone calls are hard to keep track of.

A website invitation with online RSVP puts everything in one place. Guests open a single link, see the details, answer a few questions, and you see every reply in a list. Here is how to set it up so it works for everyone, from her friends to her grandparents.

How a website invitation with RSVP works

A website invitation is a one-page site made in Canva. It holds what a printed invitation would, plus the things that never fit on a card: the schedule, a map to the church and the reception hall, the dress code, a countdown and her photos.

The RSVP part is a button on the site that opens a short form. Guests fill it in on their phone, press submit, and their answer is saved. Many families use a free Google Form for this, because it is easy to edit and the replies can go straight into a spreadsheet.

Canva can publish the site on a free link, so you do not need to buy a domain or pay for hosting. If you change something later, such as the reception time, you edit the design and publish again, and guests keep using the same link.

What to ask on the RSVP form

Keep the form short. Every extra question is a reason for someone to put it off until later. For most quinceañeras, these are enough:

  1. Name, or family name. Ask for the name as it appears on the invitation, so you can match replies to your guest list.
  2. Will you attend? A simple yes or no.
  3. How many people are coming? Give a dropdown with a small range, and tell guests how many seats are reserved for them (see the tip below).
  4. Which parts will you attend? Useful if the mass and the reception are in different places or at different times.
  5. Meal choice or dietary needs. Only if your caterer needs it.
  6. A message for the quinceañera. Optional, but she will love reading them.

Make the first three questions required and the rest optional. Write the questions in plain words, and if many guests prefer Spanish, write each question in both languages on the same line so nobody has to switch versions.

A tip for guest numbers: on the site, near the RSVP button, add a line such as "We have reserved seats for your family. Please let us know how many will attend." Some families also write the number of seats on the invitation they send to each household. A form cannot stop someone from typing a larger number, but a clear, kind note makes it far less likely.

Setting the RSVP deadline

Ask your venue and caterer when they need the final headcount, then set your RSVP deadline a week or so before that date. Many families set it about three to four weeks before the party, which leaves time to chase missing replies and finish the seating plan.

Work backwards from there. If guests need to reply three or four weeks before, the invitation should go out well ahead of that, usually around two months before the quince. Send a save the date earlier still if many guests are traveling. Our quinceañera planning timeline lays out each step, and the party planner works out the dates for you from her quince date.

Put the deadline in two places: on the website, right next to the RSVP button, and in the description of the form itself.

Sharing the link

The best part of a website invitation is how easy it is to send:

  • By text and WhatsApp. Paste the link with a short personal message. Family group chats spread it quickly.
  • With a video invitation. Send the video first, then the website link in the next message for all the details. Many families use both: the video for the excitement, the website for the practical information.
  • On social media. Add the link to stories or a private post for friends.
  • On printed cards. For guests who prefer paper, print a few cards with a QR code that opens the website. Our guide to digital or printed quinceañera invitations shows how to combine both.

If you need help with the words that go with the link, see our quinceañera invitation wording examples, including short messages for text and WhatsApp.

Before you send it to everyone, test it yourself. Open the link on an iPhone and an Android phone if you can, press the RSVP button, submit a test reply, and check that it arrives. Then delete the test reply.

Keeping track of replies

If you use a Google Form, the replies appear in the form's Responses tab, either as a summary or one by one. A few settings make life easier:

  • Link it to Google Sheets. In Responses, use the option to link to Sheets. Every reply then lands in a spreadsheet, where you can add a column for table numbers and sort by family.
  • Turn on email notifications. In Responses, under More, choose to get an email for new responses, so you know the moment someone replies.
  • Close the form after the deadline. In the form's settings you can stop accepting responses, or set it to close on a date, with a short message asking late guests to call you instead.

Share the spreadsheet with one or two people who are helping, such as a padrino or a sister, so you are not the only one checking it. Keep in mind that anyone who can edit the form may also see the linked sheet, so only share it with people you trust with guests' details.

Following up with guests who have not replied

Some guests will not reply, no matter how easy you make it. That is normal. About a week before the deadline, compare your spreadsheet with your guest list and send a friendly reminder with the link again. A short message works well: "We are finalizing the numbers for Sofia's quince. Could you let us know by Friday if you can come?"

After the deadline, call the families you still have not heard from. Grandparents and older relatives often prefer to answer by phone anyway, so it helps to have someone ready to enter their reply on the form for them. That way every answer ends up in the same list.

Making it easy for every guest

Online RSVPs work for most families, but a few small touches make sure nobody is left out:

  • Add a phone number on the website for guests who would rather call.
  • Use large, clear text and a big RSVP button. Many guests will open the site on a small phone.
  • Keep the address and the time near the top, so guests can find them on the day without scrolling.

Frequently asked questions

Do guests need an account to RSVP on a website invitation?

No. Guests simply open the link and fill in the form. With a Google Form, guests do not need to sign in unless you choose a setting that requires it, so check that sign-in is not required before you share the link.

When should the RSVP deadline be for a quinceañera?

Ask your venue and caterer when they need the final count, and set the deadline about a week before that. Many families choose a date three to four weeks before the party.

Can I change the website after I send the link?

Yes. Edit the design in Canva and publish it again. Guests keep the same link and see the updated details the next time they open it.

What if an older relative does not use the internet?

Let them answer by phone and enter their reply on the form yourself, or ask a helper to do it. That keeps every answer in one list.
