A first birthday slideshow is really a slideshow for the grown-ups: a year of firsts, played while the guest of honor is busy with cake. The good news is that the photos already exist, scattered across your phone. Here is how to turn them into a short story.

## Pick one standout photo per month

The month-by-month structure is what makes first-year slideshows work. Choose one hero photo for each month, then add two or three supporting shots.

| Month | Ideas |
|---|---|
| Newborn | Hospital photos, first time home, tiny hands and feet |
| 1 | First bath, sleeping on a parent's chest |
| 2 | First smiles, meeting grandparents |
| 3 | Tummy time, first holiday or outing |
| 4 | Laughing, grabbing toys |
| 5 | Rolling over, first time in the pool or park |
| 6 | Sitting up, first taste of food |
| 7 | Messy meals, first tooth |
| 8 | Crawling, exploring everything |
| 9 | Standing with help, first trip |
| 10 | Waving, clapping, first words |
| 11 | Cruising the furniture, first steps |
| 12 | The party, cake smash, the whole family |

## Use the milestone cards

If you took monthly milestone photos with a card or a blanket, put them at the start of each month. They make the timeline instantly clear to guests.

## Keep it short

Three to five minutes is plenty: about one song. Guests are watching between conversations, and the birthday child will not sit still for longer.

## Choose music that grows with the story

A soft lullaby or acoustic song for the newborn months, then something more upbeat as the photos get more active. Canva lets you add your own audio and trim it to the slideshow.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many photos should a first birthday slideshow have?

Around 40 to 60: one hero photo per month plus a few supporting shots, which fits a three to five minute slideshow.

### When should I start making the slideshow?

Two to three weeks before the party. Collecting the photos takes longer than editing them.
